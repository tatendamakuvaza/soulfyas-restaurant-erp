# Front Matter

## Welcome to Your New Career

**The Practical Data Analyst's Handbook — From Zero to Your First Data Job: A Complete Beginner's Course**

**Tatenda Makuvaza — Data Analyst, Author, Educator**

First Edition, 2026

### Who This Book Is For

You have never written a line of code. You open a spreadsheet and feel slightly tired. The word "statistics" reminds you of a classroom you were happy to leave. And yet somewhere you have heard that data analysis is a good, honest, well-paid skill — one that exists in every industry, one you could actually learn — and you wonder whether someone like you could really do it.

Yes. This book was written precisely for you.

It assumes **zero background**. No mathematics beyond arithmetic. No programming. No prior tools. Every term is explained the moment it appears, in plain English, with a worked example before you are asked to try anything yourself. Every tool in this book has a free version, and Chapter 4 shows you how to install all of them in one afternoon.

### The Promise

By the last page you will have:

1. **Five tools, genuinely learned** — Excel, SQL, statistics (with SPSS and Stata), Python, and Power BI — each introduced from absolute zero, each practiced on real (generated) business data.
2. **Six completed projects** — including three portfolio-ready analyses with walkthroughs — all built on the running case of a real-seeming small business.
3. **A portfolio** — a GitHub page an employer can click, showing your work, by Chapter 49.
4. **The job hunt, handled** — CV, LinkedIn, technical interviews, case interviews, take-homes, and your first 90 days once hired.
5. **The map of what comes next** — machine learning, big data, ethics, and the career decade after your first job.

### How This Book Works

**One running case.** In Chapter 5 you meet **Tariro**, who runs a grocery store in Highfield, Harare — a notebook, a half-trusted spreadsheet, and a dream of a second branch. You become her analyst, and you stay hired for the whole book. Every formula, query, and chart you learn is immediately used on her real-seeming data (generated with documented patterns, so your answers can always be checked against the truth). When you interview for your first job, Tariro is your story.

**One chapter recipe.** Every chapter tells you what you will learn, teaches it with worked examples, closes with Key Takeaways and a Practice Lab you can actually do (data provided), and points you to further reading. Short chapters, steady stairs.

**One toolkit, bridged.** The "From Your Toolkit" callouts connect every new tool back to the ones you already know — the same table-join appears first in Excel, then in SQL, then in Python — so nothing you learn is ever an island.

**One honesty habit.** Averages lie, samples have error, correlation is not causation. You will meet these truths in Part I and be held to them in every part after. The most employable thing about you will not be a tool; it will be that your numbers can be trusted.

### A Note on Cost

Everything in this book is free except Excel. If you do not own Excel, Chapter 6 introduces **LibreOffice Calc**, its free twin, and every exercise works identically in both — the few differences are flagged wherever they appear. Power BI Desktop, Python, SQLite, and the practice datasets cost nothing. Your total toolkit bill can be **zero dollars**, and Chapter 61 shows the $200 version that makes life easier. Cost is never the barrier; patience briefly is.

### From the Author

I completed a six-module data analytics course — SQL, Excel, Python, Power BI, Stata and SPSS — and I remember exactly how each of them felt on day one: the spreadsheet that would not add up, the query that returned an error, the statistics lecture that seemed to be in a foreign language. This book is the course I wish I had been handed: every tool from zero, every concept in plain words, one friendly business to practice on, and the honest career guidance at the end.

Work the labs. Be patient with yourself on day one of each new tool — everyone is. And keep Tariro's question in mind whenever the material feels abstract: *someone, somewhere, is making a decision with this number.* That is the whole job, and it is a good one.

— Tatenda Makuvaza, Harare, 2026
